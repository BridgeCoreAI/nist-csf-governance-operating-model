# Maturity Model

A five-level model used to assess how consistently and reliably a control operates, assessed only after control status has been determined.

## Level 0 , Non-Existent

No evidence, documentation, ownership, awareness, or implementation exists. Expected evidence: none. What is needed to advance: identify the requirement, assign ownership, and begin documenting the process.

## Level 1 , Ad Hoc or Reactive

The activity may occur, but inconsistently, informally, and dependent on individual effort. Expected evidence: emails, informal notes, isolated actions. What is needed to advance: create formal procedures, assign roles, document the process.

## Level 2 , Documented and Defined

The process is formally documented, approved, communicated, and supported by defined roles. Expected evidence: policies, procedures, approved records, meeting minutes, defined ownership. What is needed to advance: establish active management, recurring review, monitoring, measurable outcomes.

## Level 3 , Managed and Measured

The process is actively managed, monitored, measured, and supported by structured tools or automation where appropriate. Expected evidence: metrics, dashboards, recurring reviews, tracked actions, trend analysis. What is needed to advance: use lessons learned, trend analysis, testing results, and stakeholder feedback to improve the process.

## Level 4 , Optimized and Continuously Improved

The process is continuously improved using performance data, lessons learned, emerging risks, and stakeholder feedback. Expected evidence: trend-based improvements, adaptive controls, lessons-learned records, measurable performance gains.

Not every control requires automation to reach Level 3. Automation is one possible mechanism for management and measurement, not a requirement of the level itself.
